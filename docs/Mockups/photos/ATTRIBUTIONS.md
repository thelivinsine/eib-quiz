# Photography — sources, licences and why we may use them

Seven photographs and one map, fetched from Wikimedia Commons on 2026-09-21,
replacing the AI-generated placeholders in the mockup contact sheets. Every file here is
freely licensed for **any purpose, including commercial use**, provided the
credit line below is shown.

Files are capped at **3840px on the long edge** — far beyond any web need, and a
fraction of the originals' weight. Each entry links the original if you ever need
full resolution.

comply with only that one. Take CC BY-SA 3.0 — the GFDL is written for
software manuals and would oblige you to reproduce the full licence text.

**Why this is OK:** the German flag is a federal insignia with no copyright.
The photograph is the photographer's own work. Note that some Commons flag
files carry an `insignia` warning — this one does not, but be aware that
*non-copyright* rules exist on misuse of national symbols (§ 124 OWiG). A
citizenship-test study site showing the flag respectfully is squarely fine.

| **Licence** | **CC0** — public domain dedication, no attribution required |
| **Here** | 3840 × 2560 · original 8116 × 5411 |

**Why these are OK, and the one caveat:** the castle's architects (Eduard
Riedel, Georg von Dollmann, Julius Hofmann) all died between 1885 and 1907,
so architectural copyright expired long ago. Both photographs are freely
licensed by their photographers.

The caveat is **not** copyright but **property law**. The Bavarian Palace
Administration, like its Prussian counterpart, asserts that *commercial*
exploitation of photographs taken from within its grounds requires its
permission — a position German courts have broadly upheld for state palace
foundations (BGH, *Preußische Schlösser und Gärten*, 2010). The Marienbrücke
viewpoint sits on administered land; the village view does not.

**Practical reading:** a free, non-commercial study site is not the target of
that claim, and the primary image is fine as-is. If this project ever carries
advertising, sells anything, or is licensed to a third party, switch to
`neuschwanstein-alt-cc0.jpg`, which is shot from public ground and carries no
obligations of any kind.

## A. Freedom of panorama (§ 59 UrhG)

German copyright law permits photographing works permanently located on
public streets, ways and squares, and reproducing and distributing those
photographs **including commercially**. It applies to exteriors, seen from
publicly accessible ground level — not from a drone, a ladder, or private
property.

This is what makes photographs of modern German architecture usable at all.
Relevant here to the Reichstag, whose 1999 Foster dome is still in copyright;
the Commons file carries the explicit `{{Licensed-FOP|Germany}}` tag for that
reason. The other buildings are old enough not to need it.

## B. What share-alike actually obliges — and what it does not

Four of the six files are CC BY-SA. This trips people up, so plainly:

**You must:**
- Name the author, using the exact wording where one is specified above.
- Name the licence and link to it.
- State it if you changed the image.
- Licence **the modified photograph** under the same licence, if you crop,
  recolour, or otherwise adapt it.

**You need not:**
- Licence your website, your app, your CSS, or your question data under
  CC BY-SA. Placing an unmodified photo on a page does not make the page an
  adaptation of the photo. This is Creative Commons' own stated reading — see
  their FAQ, [When is my use considered an adaptation?](https://creativecommons.org/faq/#when-is-my-use-considered-an-adaptation)
- Pay anything, or ask permission.
- Keep the image unmodified. You may crop and edit freely; the obligation is
  only to say so and to pass the licence along on the edited photo.

**Where the credit goes:** visibly near the image, or in a credits page linked
from anywhere the image appears. This project already uses the second pattern
in [`img/ATTRIBUTIONS.md`](../../../img/ATTRIBUTIONS.md) — if any of these
photographs ship in the app, copy their credit lines there too, since that
file is what the live site's attribution points at.

## C. Why not AI-generated photographs

The mockups originally used generated images of these landmarks. Beyond
resolution, generated architecture is subtly wrong in ways a German audience
notices immediately — the Quadriga's horse count and pose, the spacing of the
Reichstag's `DEM DEUTSCHEN VOLKE` inscription, the flag's stripe order. For a
citizenship-test study aid, visual accuracy about German state symbols is part
of being credible. Real photographs also come with a clean, documented
provenance chain, which generated images do not.
